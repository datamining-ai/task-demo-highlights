## Management position

July customer invoice AIR-0731 is **12.0 gross**, with **8.0 of signed customer-claim credits** and **4.0 scheduled cash payment on 16 August**. The two cases are commercially agreed in full; their application in the payment run remains to be checked. They generate no separate cash payment and do not reduce the loan balance. The file establishes acceptance of the remaining July account items but supplies no amounts for any additional invoices.

The two signed seller separation orders total **23.5**, of which **2.0 has been paid** and **21.5 remains payable at transaction completion**. Those fixed vendor payments remain in force and are independent of invoice collections, loan balances and working-capital adjustments. They are obligations of the seller operating company, INC; they are not Airbus supplier purchase orders.

## Loan reconciliation and public-disclosure cross-check

The customer portfolio statement gives an agreed opening group balance of 122.0625 on 30 June, matching the amount in term-sheet section 1.17. The credited 12 July receipt AC0712 reduces that claim by 4.0000; the July drawings/charges line is zero. Thus **122.0625 − 4.0000 + 0 = 118.0625**. No later loan movement is supplied. The statement records the receipt as credited; a separate bank statement has not been supplied.

The Q2 filing provides context rather than a subsidiary loan ledger. Note 11 identifies an A350 advance of 100.0 between Airbus S.A.S. and Spirit Europe and an A220 advance of 17.0 between Airbus Canada and Shorts Brothers PLC. Their combined disclosed principal is 117.0, leaving **5.0625 between those two figures and the agreed 122.0625 group opening**. The disclosure dates and scope differ, and no allocation or detailed loan schedule explains this difference. Keep it as an unreconciled composition item, not an additional claim or a presumed fee. The 100.0 and 17.0 must not be added again to the group claim.

The public A350 arrangements originally contemplated 2025 delivery offsets plus a nominal fee, with any unoffset amount due on 31 December 2025. The filing also describes pledged Scotland programme assets. The subsequent 30 June term sheet requires the outstanding loan arrangements to be repaid at or before completion, and section 2.2 requires full payoff and termination as a closing condition. The public repayment description does not establish a new July loan or permit an assumed July delivery offset. Neither the opening balance nor the July repayment can be allocated across lenders from these records. Obtain the affiliate breakdown, explanation of the 5.0625 and payoff/security-release evidence in the normal account follow-up; do not estimate them.

## Accepted invoice settlements

| A350-DC-18 | 2 August | 3.0 | Full settlement through July invoice credit |
| **Total** | | **8.0** | **No separate bank payment for either case** |

The 2 August remittance advice reconciles the invoice exactly: **12.0 − 5.0 − 3.0 = 4.0**. From Airbus's perspective, the planned payment clears a 12.0 supplier invoice using 8.0 of agreed claim credits and 4.0 cash; from Spirit Europe's perspective, cash collection is 4.0. This is a customer settlement, not loan principal repayment.

At 5 August, distinguish the signed commercial settlements from their accounting and payment execution. The file contains no credit-note posting, general ledger or bank evidence for the future 16 August run. Reflect the agreed credits in the management collection expectation, have account finance confirm the appropriate July close treatment of the August agreements, and match the credits and cash when posted. The gross invoice should not simply be labelled cash collected or the 8.0 labelled a completed bank repayment.

After the advised credits are applied, these two cases have **zero residual claim**, and there is no other quantified agreed liquidated-damages balance in the simulated customer file. Until application is confirmed, track the 8.0 as agreed credits awaiting execution, not an extra future cash claim. Do not charge these cases again at transaction completion. The historical liquidated-damages amount in the public term sheet is redacted and cannot be reconstructed. This close does not establish that all potential or future claims are nil. Section 1.17 requires timely identification and good-faith agreement of further claims, with disputed amounts handled separately; continue the ordinary monthly claims review.

## Transition-purchase obligations and expense cut-off

RF-118, signed 15 July, covers production-equipment and utilities ring-fencing for Northline separation works. Its fixed price is 9.5; the vendor acknowledges receipt of the 2.0 deposit on 19 July, leaving 7.5 due at completion. No final invoice is supplied. IT-064, signed 29 July with Harbor systems services, covers migration and separation of seller IT access for 14.0; nothing has been paid, performance is before completion, and the full 14.0 is due at completion. The 2 August confirmations state that scope and prices are unchanged.

Sections 1.7 and 1.8 place seller ring-fencing and separation costs on Spirit. Both orders are identified as seller separation commitments and remain with INC in this schedule. They must not be reclassified as Airbus integration expenditure or assumed to reduce the outstanding loan. The 50.0 integration allocation is already included in the term-sheet Cash Amount; it is not another supplier allowance, nor does it eliminate these seller commitments. Airbus bears its own integration and transaction expenses under section 1.8. The records do not authorize extra supplier scope.

**Commitment, expense recognition and payment are separate.** The 23.5 is the contracted amount; 2.0 is acknowledged cash paid; 21.5 is the remaining contractual cash requirement. None of those figures alone establishes July expense or an invoiced payable. No performance certificates, accrued-cost ledger, deposit accounting or final invoices are supplied. Ask purchase administration and accounting to substantiate work delivered by 31 July, confirm any accrual or capitalization treatment and reconcile the deposit before posting. An unbilled balance must not be omitted from commitment monitoring simply because its cash trigger is later.

## Completion-related context affecting this account

The working perimeter assumes Airbus Prestwick is sold to a third party. That assumption selects the **559.0 Cash Amount delivered by Spirit with the business to Airbus**, for nominal **USD 1 consideration paid by Airbus**, under section 1.8. It does not evidence an agreed third-party price or completed sale. The Cash Amount is separate from the 118.0625 loan claim and from the seller's 21.5 remaining vendor payments. Do not offset hypothetical Prestwick proceeds or treat the 559.0 as an Airbus payment to Spirit.

The Cash Amount already includes the 50.0 integration allocation. Working-capital targets and adjustments, tax effects and any applicable Subang/Xizi adjustments remain unresolved; the term sheet specifies a zero target for Airbus receivables, not an automatic write-off of AIR-0731. No completed purchase-price calculation is supportable. Regulatory conditions, definitive documentation and the completion date are not established in the supplied records. The filing's mid-2025 merger expectation is not a confirmed payment date. Maintain the existing fixed commitments while these matters are discussed; keep any wider transaction funding assessment outside this customer close.

## Follow-up for the programme director

The supporting action schedule names suggested functional owners and event-based follow-up points; these are management recommendations, not evidence that tasks have already been assigned.

1. **Customer accounts:** confirm credit postings for both signed cases and the July accounting cut-off; match the 4.0 cash payment and 8.0 credits in the 16 August run. Investigate any variance without reopening the agreed case values.
2. **Loan account finance:** obtain the opening balance's affiliate composition, explain the 5.0625 public-disclosure difference and allocate AC0712 on evidence. Retain 118.0625 as the group balance unless supported movements arise; prepare lender-specific payoff and security-release confirmations when completion becomes actionable.
3. **Purchase administration and accounting:** obtain RF-118/IT-064 delivery evidence, final invoices and deposit-ledger reconciliation; establish the July accrual/capitalization position. Keep 21.5 in the completion payment diary, with no additional scope assumed.
4. **Commercial claims team:** keep the agreed 8.0 visible until application is confirmed, then close both cases. Review newly identified claims within the section 1.17 notice timetable; do not forecast unsupported amounts.
5. **Programme commercial finance:** refresh the account and purchase schedules when completion timing or perimeter is confirmed. Escalate missing working-capital/tax terms and the unagreed Prestwick sale to the relevant transaction owners only as dependencies; they do not suspend existing orders.
